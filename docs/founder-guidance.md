# Founder guidance applied to PSXL

The homepage direction is grounded in current, first-party startup guidance:

- Paul Graham, “Do Things that Don’t Scale”: early startups should recruit users manually, focus on a narrow market, and make the user experience excellent while learning directly from early customers. https://www.paulgraham.com/ds.html
- Y Combinator, “YC’s Essential Startup Advice”: launch with a quantum of utility, talk to users, iterate quickly, find a 90/10 solution, and focus on a small group of customers who love the product before scaling. https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/ycs-essential-startup-advice/
- Sam Altman, “Startup Playbook”: long-term growth comes from a great product and a fast product-improvement loop driven by observing users; keep the product simple and maintain momentum. https://playbook.samaltman.com/
- Sam Altman, “Before Growth”: do not optimize premature growth targets before users love the product; first prove that users would be genuinely disappointed if the product disappeared. https://blog.samaltman.com/before-growth

PSXL application of these principles: lead with one acute promise—turn fragmented financial sources into a verified, explainable ledger—show the Connect → Sync → Review → Ledger workflow, make the source and freshness visible, and invite early customers into a focused broker/investor workflow rather than claiming generic autonomous finance.
